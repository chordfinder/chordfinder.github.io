How the Browser-Based Detection Works

An overview of how digital signal processing converts acoustic audio into musical harmony.

Phase 1 • Signal Windowing

Hann-Windowed Framing

The mono audio buffer is split into overlapping 4096-sample windows (~93 ms) with a 2048-sample hop (~46 ms). A Hann window function tapers frame boundaries to suppress spectral edge leakage before Fourier analysis.

Phase 2 • Spectral Transformation

Radix-2 Cooley-Tukey FFT

A custom radix-2 Fast Fourier Transform decomposes time-domain audio frames into frequency magnitude bins. Parabolic peak interpolation sharpens pitch estimates across musical fundamentals and overtones.

Phase 3 • Pitch-Class Folding

12-Bin Chromagram Extraction

Frequencies spanning 6 musical octaves (A1 ~55 Hz to C7 ~2100 Hz) are folded into the 12 chromatic semitones (C through B). The resulting 12-dimensional chroma vector is L2-normalized to represent pitch energy balance.

Phase 4 • Pattern Matching

Cosine Template Matching & Smoothing

Each chroma vector is compared against normalized major and minor triad profiles using cosine similarity. A temporal smoothing filter eliminates rapid transient flicker before adjacent matching frames merge into timed segments.

Accuracy Considerations & Limitations

Understanding acoustic factors that influence automated chord extraction accuracy.

Dense Mixes & Multi-Tracking

Recordings featuring multiple simultaneous instruments, distorted guitars, heavy synths, or thick reverberation produce dense overtone spectra. Overlapping frequencies can mask the underlying triad root and produce brief misidentifications.

Heavy Percussion & Drums

Sharp percussive transients like snare hits and crash cymbals disperse acoustic energy across all pitch classes as broadband noise. The engine filters out short transient clicks, but intense drum passages may temporarily register as unclassified (N.C.).

Extended Chords & Inversions

When songs employ jazz chords (major 7ths, 9ths, diminished, or augmented) or slash chords with non-root bass notes, the algorithm maps them to the nearest matching major or minor triad, or marks them ambiguous if energy is dispersed.

Recordings with Highest Accuracy

ChordFinder performs most reliably on clear, harmonically focused recordings—such as acoustic guitar, clean piano, keyboard stems, or isolated song accompaniment where harmonic instruments are prominent.

Client-Side Privacy Guarantee

Your audio files and music data remain strictly on your local device.

No Server Uploads

Audio decoding and DSP algorithms run in your browser's local memory via the Web Audio API. Zero audio bytes, filenames, or waveforms are transmitted over the network.

In-Memory Ephemeral Storage

Audio buffers exist solely in volatile memory while the application tab is active. Removing the file or refreshing the page clears all audio data immediately.

Zero Telemetry or Tracking

ChordFinder contains no tracking beacons, third-party analytics scripts, or advertising trackers. No profiling or cookie logging takes place.

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to common questions about ChordFinder's chord detection and audio capabilities.

Does ChordFinder upload my audio files?

No. All decoding and digital signal processing occur entirely in your local browser using the Web Audio API. No audio data ever leaves your computer or mobile device.

What audio formats can I analyze?

ChordFinder supports MP3, WAV, FLAC, OGG, M4A, AAC, and WebM audio files up to 50 MB in size.

Which chords can the tool identify?

The engine detects all 12 major and 12 minor triads (24 templates), as well as unpitched or silent sections (N.C.). Complex chords (such as 7ths or 9ths) are estimated by their underlying triad base.

What does "N.C." mean in the results?

"N.C." stands for No Chord. It indicates segments where the signal was silent, dominated by unpitched percussion, or lacked clear triad harmonic energy above detection thresholds.

Can I seek audio playback by clicking chords?

Yes. Clicking any chord card in the progression list or clicking any colored segment in the synchronized timeline seeks the audio player to that exact moment.

Is ChordFinder completely free to use?

Yes. ChordFinder is a free, privacy-first web utility that requires no subscription, account registration, or software download.
